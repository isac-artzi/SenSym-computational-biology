10x Genomics The company whose droplet-based platform produced the PBMC 3k dataset used in this project. A cell is co-encapsulated in an oil droplet with a bead carrying a cell barcode; captured mRNA molecules get that barcode plus a UMI, and the result is a matrix of counts per gene per cell. Used here as the source of real data, not as a subject of study. 1 · 10realdata.load_pbmc
$D$ The sequencing depth: the number of molecules captured from one cell. The independent variable of Aim 1 and Aim 2. 1depth= in simulate.simulate_cells
$d'$ ("d-prime") The separation index: the gap between two distributions' means divided by their (pooled) standard deviation. For Poisson counts $d' = |\lambda_1 - \lambda_2| / \sqrt{\bar\lambda}$. The central quantity of the project, because it is dimensionless, it grows as $\sqrt{\text{data}}$, and accuracy is a known function of it. Borrowed from 1950s detection theory, where it means exactly the same thing. 3detection.d_prime, detection.d_prime_total, beads.bead_d_prime, realdata.d_prime_overdispersed
$k$ The number of informative marker genes combined. The second independent variable of Aim 1: $d' \propto \sqrt{k}$ under independence. (Also used as a count of successes in interval formulas; context disambiguates.) 5n_genes in simulate.marker_profiles
$\lambda$ A Poisson rate — here, the expected count of a gene in a cell, $\lambda = D p$. Not a probability; it can be any positive number. 1lam throughout
$n$ In the bead experiment, the number of beads drawn in one trial — the analogue of depth. Elsewhere, a sample size. 6n in beads.*
$q$ The dropout (discard) probability. The keep probability is $1-q$. Getting these the wrong way round is the single most common sign error in the project. 9dropout= in simulate.apply_dropout
$\Phi$ (capital Phi) The standard normal cumulative distribution function: $\Phi(z) = P(Z \le z)$ for $Z\sim\mathcal N(0,1)$. Increasing from 0 to 1, with $\Phi(0)=\tfrac12$. Appears in the project's headline result, accuracy $=\Phi(d'/2)$. 4detection.normal_cdf, CD.Phi
$\varphi$ (lower-case phi) The negative-binomial dispersion parameter, defined by $\mathrm{Var} = m + \varphi m^{2}$. A completely different object from $\Phi$; see the confused pairs. 11phi in realdata.d_prime_overdispersed

A

TermDefinitionWeekCode
accuracy The fraction of cells (or trials) classified correctly. With two equally likely classes, chance is $0.5$ and the theoretical best is $\Phi(d'/2)$. Saturates at 1, which is why the project converts it back to $d'$ before fitting anything. 4classify.accuracy, detection.accuracy_from_d_prime
aim One of the project's three strands: Aim 1 (theory, Weeks 1–5), Aim 2 (simulation and real data, Weeks 7–11), Aim 3 (the physical bead analog, Weeks 6–12). Each aim can fail independently, which is the point of having three. 1—
ambient RNA Free-floating mRNA in the cell suspension that gets captured into a droplet along with (or instead of) a cell's own molecules, adding a background that belongs to no particular cell. One of the real-data complications the bead experiment deliberately does not model. 6 · 10—
AUC (area under the ROC curve) The probability that a randomly chosen cell of type 2 receives a higher score than a randomly chosen cell of type 1. Threshold-free, so it measures the data's separability rather than a particular decision rule. Under the Gaussian model $\mathrm{AUC} = \Phi(d'/\sqrt2)$. 4detection.roc_auc, detection.d_prime_from_auc

B

TermDefinitionWeekCode
barcode A short synthetic DNA sequence attached to every molecule captured from one droplet, so that after everything is pooled and sequenced you can tell which cell each read came from. Cell barcodes identify cells; UMIs identify molecules. 10—
Bayes error The lowest error rate achievable by any classifier given the true distributions. It is a property of the data, not of an algorithm. $\Phi(d'/2)$ is a Gaussian approximation to $1 - $ Bayes error; for Poisson counts the exact value can be computed by summation. 4detection.bayes_error_exact
Bernoulli trial A single yes/no experiment with success probability $p$. The atom from which the binomial is built: $n$ independent Bernoulli trials summed give a binomial. 2—
binomial distribution The number of successes in $n$ independent trials each with probability $p$. Mean $np$, variance $np(1-p)$. Describes the beads exactly; describes gene counts too, but is indistinguishable from Poisson when $p$ is tiny and $n$ is large. 2CD.binomial (labs)
blinding Withholding the true answer from the person making the guess, so that their guess cannot be influenced by it. In Aim 3 the guesser must not see which jar, must get no feedback within a block, and must not read the helper's reactions. 6bead protocol §4
bootstrap Estimating the uncertainty of a statistic by resampling your own data with replacement, recomputing the statistic each time, and looking at the spread. Used here to put an interval on a fitted exponent, where no closed-form standard error is available. 13stats.bootstrap_ci

C

TermDefinitionWeekCode
cell type Biologically, a category of cell defined by what it does and expresses. For this project, a probability distribution over which gene the next transcript belongs to — a vector $p^{(t)}$ summing to 1. Reducing a cell type to a fixed vector is a simplification, and it is the one that breaks in Week 11. 1simulate.marker_profiles
central limit theorem Sums (and averages) of many independent contributions tend toward a normal distribution, regardless of what the individual contributions look like. It is what licenses treating Poisson counts as Gaussian, and therefore what puts the $\Phi$ into $\Phi(d'/2)$. 4—
chance level The accuracy of guessing without using the data: $0.5$ for two equally likely classes. Any accuracy claim must be read against it, and any measured accuracy at or below it converts to $d' \le 0$ and cannot go on a log plot. 1—
claim ledger The project's running table in which every claim appears with its measured value, its predicted value, its interval, and its status label. Built in Week 13 and carried into the report. Its purpose is to make "the data agrees with the theory" and "I want the data to agree with the theory" impossible to blur. 13progress/week13_claims.md
claim status labels The four tags every claim carries. proved you can derive it at a whiteboard, on demand, without notes. cited someone else established it and you give a checkable reference. observed measured here, with a seed and an interval, but not derived. conjectured believed, and stated plainly as a belief. An unlabelled claim is not admissible. 1 · 13.claim CSS classes
classifier Any rule that takes a cell's counts and returns a predicted type. In this project the classifier is deliberately not the interesting part: all reasonable classifiers converge to the same ceiling, so the project studies the ceiling. 8celldetect.classify
confidence interval A range of values consistent with your data at a stated confidence level. "95%" refers to the procedure's long-run coverage, not to a probability that this particular interval contains the truth. Every proportion in this project gets one. 13stats.wilson_interval
cross-validation Splitting the data into $n$ folds, training on $n-1$ and testing on the remaining one, rotating through all folds, and averaging. Uses every cell for both training and testing without ever testing on a cell that was trained on. Any feature selection must happen inside each fold. 8classify.cross_validated_accuracy

D

TermDefinitionWeekCode
decision boundary The surface in feature space separating the region where a classifier says "type 1" from where it says "type 2". For the Poisson log-likelihood ratio it is a plane (the rule is linear in the counts), which is why logistic regression can match naive Bayes exactly in principle. 8—
depth The number of mRNA molecules captured and counted from one cell — the project's measure of "how much data per cell". Distinct from reads and from the observed library size; see the confused pairs. 1depth= in simulate.*
depth ceiling (the knee) Under overdispersion, $d'$ stops growing with depth and saturates at $|\Delta p| / (\bar p \sqrt{\varphi})$. The knee is where the transition happens, at a mean count of $\bar m = 1/\varphi$. Past it, deeper sequencing buys essentially nothing. Week 11's headline result. 11realdata.d_prime_overdispersed
dispersion ($\varphi$) The parameter measuring how much a gene's variance exceeds the Poisson floor: $\mathrm{Var} = m + \varphi m^{2}$. Unlike the Fano factor it does not change with sequencing depth, which is why it is the quantity to report. 11realdata.negative_binomial_dispersion
downsampling Discarding a random fraction of a dataset's molecules to imitate a shallower experiment. Legitimate because of the thinning theorem: the result has exactly the distribution a genuinely shallower run would have had, under the Poisson model. 2 · 10counting.downsample_counts, realdata.downsample_matrix
dropout The loss of captured molecules before they are counted, modelled as each molecule being discarded independently with probability $q$. If the loss does not depend on which gene a molecule belongs to, dropout is exactly equivalent to sequencing at depth $D(1-q)$ — nothing more. 9simulate.apply_dropout

TermDefinitionWeekCode
effective depth $D_{\text{eff}} = D(1-q)$: the depth that a dropout-affected measurement is statistically equivalent to. Plotting a dropout arm against effective depth rather than nominal depth should make it fall on top of the no-dropout curve, and that collapse is the testable prediction. 9 · 12dropout= in beads.bead_d_prime
expectation ($E[X]$) The probability-weighted average of a random variable — its long-run mean. Adds across sums whether or not the variables are independent, which is the asymmetry with variance that the whole $\sqrt k$ law turns on. 2counting.empirical_moments
expression How much of a gene's mRNA a cell contains. In this project always as a fraction $p_g$ of the transcriptome, so that a count is $\mathrm{Poisson}(Dp_g)$ and depth is separated from biology. 1realdata.estimate_rates

TermDefinitionWeekCode
fallback ladder The plan's pre-specified sequence of scope reductions, F1–F4, describing in advance how the project contracts if a milestone slips. Written before the pressure arrives so that the decision is not made under it. plans/ · 9—
Fano factor Variance divided by mean. Exactly 1 for Poisson data; greater than 1 means overdispersed. Depends on depth, so a Fano factor quoted without the mean it was measured at is not a reportable number. 1counting.fano_factor, realdata.gene_fano

TermDefinitionWeekCode
gene A stretch of DNA transcribed into mRNA. For this project, simply an index: one of the ~20,000 categories a captured molecule can fall into. 1columns of X
generative model A model that says how the data was produced, and can therefore be run forward to make new data. "Type $t$ has rate vector $p^{(t)}$; draw $D$ molecules" is a generative model. TermDefinitionWeekCode
hypergeometric distribution The count of successes when drawing without replacement from a finite pool. Its variance is smaller than the binomial's by the finite-population factor $(N-n)/(N-1)$. Relevant because a bead scoop is technically hypergeometric, which the protocol keeps negligible by using small scoops. 6bead protocol §5

K · L

TermDefinitionWeekCode
keep probability The probability a molecule survives thinning: $c = 1 - q$. The functions in this project are split between the two conventions on purpose, and the docstrings say which — read them. 2keep_prob= in counting.downsample_counts; CD.thin(count, keep, r)
k-nearest neighbours (kNN) Classify a cell by the majority label among its $k$ closest training cells. Makes no assumption about the count distribution, which is what makes it a useful check: if kNN beats naive Bayes, your generative model is wrong. 8classify.knn_predict
leakage Any route by which information about the test set reaches the model before evaluation. The classic case here is selecting marker genes using all the cells' labels and only then splitting into train and test — which inflates accuracy and looks like success. 10realdata.select_markers (call inside the training fold)
library size The total number of counted molecules in one cell — the row sum of the count matrix. The observed quantity corresponding to the modelled depth $D$; the two differ, and Week 10 is where that starts to matter. 1 · 10counting.total_counts
likelihood The probability of the data you observed, viewed as a function of the parameters rather than of the data. Not a probability distribution over parameters — that would be a posterior. 4 · 5counting.poisson_logpmf
log-likelihood ratio (LLR) $\log\big(P(\text{data}\mid\text{type 2}) / P(\text{data}\mid\text{type 1})\big)$. The optimal statistic for a two-class decision: threshold it at 0 (with equal priors) and you have the Bayes-optimal rule. For Poisson counts it is linear in the counts, which is the fact that makes the entire theory tractable. 5detection.log_likelihood_ratio
logistic regression A classifier that fits a linear score and squashes it through the logistic function to get a probability. Since the optimal rule here is already linear, logistic regression can in principle match it exactly; any gap is a finite-sample effect and is a result, not a bug. 8classify.LogisticRegression

N · O

TermDefinitionWeekCode
naive Bayes A classifier that assumes the features are conditionally independent given the class, so the log-likelihood ratio is a sum of per-gene terms. "Naive" refers to that independence assumption. With the true Poisson rates it is the Bayes-optimal rule, which is why it is the project's baseline. 5classify.naive_bayes_predict, detection.posterior_log_odds
negative binomial A count distribution with an extra parameter beyond the mean, arising naturally as a Poisson whose rate is itself gamma-distributed (a gamma–Poisson mixture). Variance $m + \varphi m^{2}$. The RNA-seq field's standard model, because real replicate counts are consistently more variable than Poisson. 11realdata.d_prime_overdispersed, CD.nbLogRatio
normalization Rescaling each cell's counts to remove differences in library size, so that cells are comparable. Necessary in practice and slightly dangerous in theory, because it removes a shared factor whose presence is part of what overdispersion measures. 10—
null hypothesis The default claim a test tries to reject — typically "there is no effect". Rejecting it is evidence for something; failing to reject it is not evidence that it is true, especially with the sample sizes in this project. 13stats.permutation_test
overdispersion Variance exceeding what the counting model predicts (Fano factor above 1). In scRNA-seq the cause is largely biological: cells of the "same type" are not identical, so the rate itself varies from cell to cell. Week 11's central finding, and the reason the $\sqrt{D}$ law has a ceiling. 11realdata.gene_fano
overfitting Learning features of the training sample that do not generalize. Diagnosed by a gap between training and test accuracy; 1-NN is the extreme case, scoring 100% on its own training data by construction. 8classify.train_test_split

TermDefinitionWeekCode
PBMC (peripheral blood mononuclear cell) The round-nucleus white blood cells — T cells, B cells, monocytes, NK cells. Easy to obtain from a blood sample and easy to dissociate, which is why "PBMC 3k" is single-cell genomics' equivalent of a standard test image. 10realdata.load_pbmc
permutation test Shuffle the labels many times, recompute your statistic each time, and see where the real value sits in that distribution. Assumption-light, and doubles as a bug detector: a pipeline that scores above chance on shuffled labels has leakage. 13stats.permutation_test
Poisson distribution The count of rare independent events at rate $\lambda$: $P(X=k) = \lambda^{k}e^{-\lambda}/k!$. Mean $\lambda$ and — the coincidence the whole project rests on — variance $\lambda$ too, so the relative noise is $1/\sqrt\lambda$. 1counting.sample_counts, counting.poisson_pmf, CD.poisson
posterior The probability of each class after seeing the data: prior times likelihood, normalized. What a classifier should ideally report instead of a bare label. 5 · 9detection.posterior_log_odds
power (statistical) The probability that an experiment detects a real effect of a given size. Computed before running, from the effect size and the sample size. A design without adequate power is not an experiment; it is a way of spending a month. 6beads.trials_needed
prior The probability of each class before seeing any data. Taken as $\tfrac12$ each throughout, which is what makes the optimal threshold sit exactly halfway and produces the factor of 2 in $\Phi(d'/2)$. 4priors= in detection.posterior_log_odds

TermDefinitionWeekCode
UMI (unique molecular identifier) A random tag attached to each captured molecule before amplification, so that many reads from one molecule can be collapsed back to a count of one. UMI counts, not read counts, are what "molecules captured" means in this project. 10—

Commonly confused pairs

Six distinctions that account for most of the confusion in this project. Each one has cost somebody a week.

1  $\Phi$ versus $\varphi$

$\Phi$ (capital) is the standard normal cumulative distribution function. It is a function, it takes a $z$-score, it returns a probability between 0 and 1, and it appears in accuracy $= \Phi(d'/2)$. detection.normal_cdf.

$\varphi$ (lower-case) is the negative-binomial dispersion parameter. It is a number, it is a property of a gene, it is typically between 0 and a few, and it appears in $\mathrm{Var} = m + \varphi m^{2}$. realdata.negative_binomial_dispersion.

They have nothing to do with each other and the collision is purely notational. Be aware also that in many statistics texts lower-case $\phi$ means the normal density (the derivative of $\Phi$) — a third meaning. When you write for a reader outside this project, define the symbol.

2  Depth versus library size versus read count

Depth $D$ is the model parameter: the number of molecules captured from a cell. It is what you set in the simulator and what the theory's $\sqrt D$ refers to.

Library size is the observed row sum of the count matrix for a cell — the number of UMIs actually recorded. It is the empirical stand-in for $D$, and it varies enormously between cells, which is itself a source of overdispersion.

Read count is what you buy from the sequencing facility. Several reads collapse to one UMI after deduplication, so reads exceed molecules, by a factor that depends on how deeply the library was sequenced. Vendor depth recommendations are quoted in reads per cell, which is not your $D$.

Practical consequence: "we sequenced at 50,000 reads per cell" and "each cell contributes 3,000 molecules" can both be true of the same experiment. When you plot against depth, say which one you mean, in the axis label.

3  Accuracy versus $d'$

Accuracy is bounded: it lives in $[0.5, 1]$ for a two-class problem and it saturates. Equal changes in the underlying separability produce wildly unequal changes in accuracy depending on where you are — from 0.55 to 0.60 is a small change in $d'$, from 0.98 to 0.99 is a large one.

$d'$ is unbounded and scales cleanly: it doubles when the data quadruples, always, everywhere on the range.

Consequence, and it is the methodological hinge of Week 12: fit exponents in $d'$ space, never in accuracy space. Fitting a power law to accuracy returns a number ($0.104$ for the 30/35 jars) that is not an estimate of anything and that changes when you change the beads. Convert first: $d' = 2\Phi^{-1}(\text{accuracy})$.

Second consequence: only $d'$ is comparable across experiments with different units of data. Accuracy of 0.7 on beads and accuracy of 0.7 on cells are not the same statement about anything.

4  Dropout $q$ versus keep probability $c$

$c = 1 - q$. Trivially. And yet this is the most frequent sign error in the codebase, because the two conventions coexist on purpose:

FunctionArgumentMeaning of a value of 0.3
counting.downsample_counts(counts, keep_prob, rng)keep_probkeep 30%, lose 70%
realdata.downsample_matrix(X, keep_prob, rng)keep_probkeep 30%
simulate.apply_dropout(X, dropout, rng)dropoutlose 30%, keep 70%
beads.bead_d_prime(n, p_a, p_b, dropout)dropoutlose 30%
CD.thin(count, keep, r)keepkeep 30%

The signature of getting it backwards: your dropout curve is above the no-dropout curve. Dropout can only ever cost you data, so if losing molecules improved your accuracy, you inverted a probability. Check this before checking anything else.

5  Fano factor versus dispersion

Fano factor $F = \mathrm{Var}/\mathrm{mean}$. Equals 1 for Poisson. It depends on depth: for a gamma–Poisson gene, $F = 1 + \varphi m$, so a gene measured at half the depth reports a smaller $F$ on identical biology. Quoting "$F = 3.4$" without saying at what mean count is not a reportable number.

Dispersion $\varphi$, from $\mathrm{Var} = m + \varphi m^{2}$. Equals 0 for Poisson. It does not depend on depth — thinning a gamma–Poisson leaves $\varphi$ unchanged while shrinking $F$ (problem B.4 in the problem bank proves it).

So: use $F$ as a diagnostic — "is this gene Poisson?" — and quote $\varphi$ as a result. And notice that only $\varphi$ can tell you where the depth ceiling is, because the knee sits at $m = 1/\varphi$.

6  Variance versus standard error

Variance (and its square root, the standard deviation) describes how spread out the data is. It does not shrink when you collect more data — a deeper sample of a wide distribution is still wide.

Standard error describes how uncertain an estimate is. It shrinks as $1/\sqrt n$, because an average of $n$ things is less variable than one thing.

The test: ask what would happen if you had a million cells. The variance of gene expression across cells would be unchanged. The standard error on your estimate of the mean would be tiny. Two different questions, two different quantities, and an error bar on a figure should say which one it is.

A third relative to keep separate: the standard deviation of a statistic, such as the spread of Fano factors you would report from repeated 50-cell samples (Week 1's Lab 2). That is a standard error too — of the Fano factor — even though it is computed from variances.
